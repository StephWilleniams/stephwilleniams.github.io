Swimmers (modelled on the alga Chlamydomonas) and passive particles share a narrow channel. Near a wall, each swimmer can be kicked: a randomly timed turn that steers it away from the wall. How often and how hard those kicks are decides where the passive particles end up.

Swimmers settle within seconds; the passive particles take thousands of simulated seconds, so switch to Max and give it a minute. Compare No kicks with Frequent, hard, and watch the running average settle onto the paper's value.

This is the model from our paper Near-boundary active kicks control passive distributions in confined active–passive suspensions (preprint coming soon), run in full: 25 three-disc swimmers and 80 Brownian passive particles in a 2000 × 6 periodic channel, WCA contacts, anisotropic drag and the same time step (dt = 6.25×10−4 s) as the paper. Lengths are in swimmer half-lengths (≈10 µm). Ep uses the paper's wall window (|y| ≥ 1.569, uniform value 0.357), counted from a plain histogram rather than the paper's smoothed estimate.
