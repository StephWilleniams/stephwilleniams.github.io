## The system

*Cassiopea*, the upside-down jellyfish, rests bell-down on the seafloor in shallow tropical water. Like corals and *Exaiptasia*, it lives with photosynthetic algae, which makes it another window onto how symbioses respond to a changing environment.

Its life cycle has two stages: a small, stationary polyp and the free-swimming jellyfish. The switch between them, called strobilation, is when a polyp transforms and releases a juvenile jellyfish. When that happens is a key life-history event, but timing it by eye is slow and inconsistent.

## The project

With Kaden Muffett, I am training deep-learning object detectors on daily photographs of *Cassiopea* polyps to measure the timing of strobilation automatically. The goal is a reliable, scalable measurement that can be applied across many animals and experimental conditions.
