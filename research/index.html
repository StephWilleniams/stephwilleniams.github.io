Research

Modeling symbiosis under stress

Most animals and plants depend on microbial partners, and many of those partnerships are sensitive to warming. Failure can start at any scale: in the flows a host uses to recruit its microbes, in how the microbes adapt, or in how host populations cope. I build mechanistic models at each of these scales, then use sensitivity and identifiability analysis to show experimental collaborators which measurements matter most.

Approach

I combine low-Reynolds-number fluid dynamics, population and trait-structured models, and data-driven inference, including machine-learning image analysis. The same toolkit runs through every project: build a model mechanistic enough to explain why a partnership breaks down, then ask which parameters can actually be measured, and which experiments would pin them down, so it can predict when.

All of this work is done in close collaboration with experimental labs: the Heath-Heckman lab (Michigan State), the Nishiguchi lab (UC Merced) and the Sogin lab (UC Riverside), through NSF INSITE, the Institute for Symbiotic Interactions, Training, and Education in the Face of a Changing Climate.
