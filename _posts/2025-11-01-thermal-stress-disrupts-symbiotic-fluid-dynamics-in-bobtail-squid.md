**Update (2026):** now published in the *Journal of the Royal Society Interface*.

Our new paper models the flows inside the mantle cavity of the Hawaiian bobtail squid, *Euprymna scolopes*. Newly hatched squid must capture the bacterium *Vibrio fischeri* from seawater, and they do it by breathing: water drawn into the mantle cavity carries bacteria past the light organ, where they are captured.

Using the method of regularized Stokeslets and a Sobol sensitivity analysis, we found that breathing strength, breathing rate and squid size control how long bacteria stay near the light organ. The shallow, rapid breathing of heat-stressed squid can halve that time, reducing young squid's ability to recruit a partner that is essential to their survival.

Williams, S., Ruiz, K. A., Heath-Heckman, E., Rutter, E. M., Khatri, S. *J. R. Soc. Interface* 23(240), 20251136 (2026).
